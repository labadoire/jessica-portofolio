PROJECT 05 / OPERATIONS ANALYTICS

ABC–XYZ Inventory Analysis

Analyzing product value and demand variability to develop inventory strategies that balance stock availability, operational efficiency, and warehouse capacity.

FIELD

Operations Analytics · Supply Chain

METHOD

ABC Classification · XYZ Analysis

FOCUS

Inventory Optimization · SKU Segmentation

01 / OVERVIEW

Managing inventory complexity through analytics.

This case study examines inventory challenges faced by Relaxo, a footwear company managing approximately 10,000 stock-keeping units (SKUs).

Despite growing inventory levels, the company experienced declining service performance, forecasting difficulties, and increasing pressure on warehouse capacity.

The analysis applies ABC–XYZ classification to a dataset of 150 SKUs to identify product priorities and recommend differentiated inventory management strategies.

02 / BUSINESS CHALLENGES

More inventory does not always mean better service.

03 / METHODOLOGY

Segmenting inventory by value and demand.

ABC–XYZ analysis combines two classification methods to understand the economic importance and demand variability of individual products.

  • ABC Analysis — classifies products according to cumulative sales value. Category A represents the first 80%, B the next 15%, and C the final 5%.
  • XYZ Analysis — evaluates demand stability using the coefficient of variation (CV), distinguishing stable, moderately variable, and highly variable demand.
  • ABC–XYZ Matrix — combines both classifications into nine inventory segments to support more targeted stock policies.

Identifying inventory priorities.

  • Category A contains only 20% of the analyzed SKUs but contributes 80% of total sales value.
  • Most analyzed products have relatively stable demand, suggesting that different stock policies are needed across segments.
  • Segment AX contains 17 high-value, stable-demand SKUs that should receive strong replenishment priority.
  • Segment AZ includes seven high-value products with volatile demand, requiring closer monitoring and flexible replenishment.
  • Segment CZ contains 12 low-value, highly variable products that are candidates for assortment review.

06 / BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS

Turning inventory insights into action.

The analysis recommends differentiated inventory policies rather than applying a uniform service level to every SKU.

  • Prioritize AX products with higher availability targets and reliable replenishment schedules.
  • Monitor AY and AZ products more frequently and adjust stock levels based on actual demand patterns.
  • Maintain balanced stock policies for B-category items according to demand stability.
  • Review low-contribution, volatile products such as CZ for reduced stocking or order-on-demand strategies.
  • Improve coordination between Sales, Supply Chain, and Finance to align inventory decisions.

These are recommendations from the case analysis, not measured outcomes from an implemented inventory policy.

07 / PROJECT OUTCOME

Using analytics to support operational decisions.

This case study demonstrates how ABC–XYZ segmentation can help businesses distinguish high-priority inventory from lower-contribution products and design more targeted inventory management strategies.

The project strengthened my understanding of demand variability, inventory segmentation, operational trade-offs, and translating analytical findings into business recommendations.
